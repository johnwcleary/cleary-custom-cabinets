We build custom cabinets in three materials: wood, thermofoil and Formica. They wear differently and they are priced differently, so the right one depends on the room and how hard it gets used.

## Wood

Wood is what most people picture: doors and frames of maple, cherry, walnut or another hardwood, stained to show the grain or painted.

Each species behaves its own way. Maple has a tight, even grain and takes paint well. Cherry starts out light and darkens over its first few years as light reaches it. Walnut is dark to begin with and usually gets a clear finish.

Wood can be repaired. A scratch can be touched up, and a whole kitchen can be refinished years later. Its drawback is that it moves. A painted door built from five pieces of wood will show hairline cracks in the paint at the joints as the seasons change. That is normal, and it is one reason some people choose thermofoil for a painted look.

## Thermofoil

Thermofoil is a vinyl film pressed with heat and vacuum onto a door cut from MDF. The film wraps the face and edges in one piece, so there are no joints to open up. The color is even from door to door, and the surface wipes clean.

It generally costs less than painted wood and comes in solid colors and wood grains. Its weak point is heat. Next to an oven, or above a toaster or coffee maker, the film can loosen over time, so those spots need a heat shield or some extra space. A damaged thermofoil door is replaced, since it can't be refinished.

## Formica and other laminates

Formica is a brand name for high-pressure laminate: sheets of paper and resin pressed into a hard, thin skin that is glued to a panel. Wilsonart and Pionite make it too, and we work with several of these brands.

Laminate doors are flat slabs, so the style is modern by nature. The range of colors and patterns is enormous, including wood grains like zebrawood that would be expensive in the real thing. The surface is hard to scratch and easy to disinfect, which is why you see it in doctors' offices and on reception desks. Wear shows first at the edges, where one sheet meets another.

## Where each one goes

For a traditional kitchen with raised-panel or Shaker doors, wood is the natural choice. For a white kitchen on a budget, or a bathroom vanity that gets wiped down every day, thermofoil is worth a look. For flat modern doors, or a space that takes abuse such as an office or a laundry room, laminate holds up best.

A kitchen can also use more than one, with wood on the island and thermofoil on the perimeter, for example.
